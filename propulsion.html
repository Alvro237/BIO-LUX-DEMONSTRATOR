02 / OVERVIEW

High delta-V capability in a 1U envelope.

To exploit affordable commercial rideshare launches, BIO-LUX must act as its own space tug.

Assuming an injection altitude of 575 km, the platform requires substantial $\Delta V$ to perform orbit acquisition up to the 674 km target, conduct station keeping to maintain the 3-day repeating ground track, and subsequently spiral down to a 360 km disposal altitude to guarantee reentry within 5 years.

Required ΔV (Worst Case) 271.1 m/s
Engine Volume ~0.8U
Nominal Firing Power 45 W

06 / DESIGN DECISIONS

Design decisions

Electric Propulsion (FEEP) Over Chemical

Chemical propulsion was rejected due to structural constraints. To achieve the 271 m/s baseline, a chemical system would require nearly 1 kg of propellant and associated tanks, breaking the 1U volume allowance. The Enpulsion Nano R3 provides massive total impulse within 0.8U using solid, unpressurized Indium.

High-Thrust Duty Cycle Selection

While the Nano R3 can operate at a higher Isp (2960 s) with lower thrust, this mode would require burn durations (e.g., 53 minutes) that exceed the battery's depth-of-discharge limit and encroach on the orbital eclipse. The high-thrust mode (350 µN, 2500 s, 45W) allows completing maneuvers inside a safe 28-minute firing window.

Continuous Magnetic Compensation

During orbit injection burns, parasitic torques from center-of-mass offset and thrust misalignment arise. Rather than relying exclusively on Reaction Wheels (which would quickly saturate), the Magnetorquers possess sufficient authority to provide continuous magnetic compensation, mitigating interruptions for desaturation.

Plume Contamination Management

Because Indium is solid at room temperature and the FEEP emitter generates a narrow beam cone (~49°), backflow is minimal (<1%). Camera lens degradation is analytically bounded to ~3.17% transmittance loss over 3 years, eliminating the need for complex deployable baffles.
